Finnish Sauna Etiquette

Master the unspoken rules of Finnish sauna culture — from proper conduct to the sacred tradition of löyly.

The Unwritten Rules of the Finnish Sauna

In Finland, sauna etiquette is passed down through generations — rarely written but universally understood. These customs ensure that everyone can enjoy a safe, respectful, and relaxing sauna experience. Whether you are visiting a public sauna, a private home, or a luxury spa in Finland, understanding these norms is essential for cultural respect.

Before Entering the Sauna

Always shower thoroughly before entering the sauna. Remove all makeup, lotions, and perfumes. In private homes and traditional public saunas, nudity is standard — a clean towel is always used to sit on for hygiene. In mixed-gender public saunas, swimwear or a towel wrap is typically worn. Remove all jewelry, as metal heats quickly and can burn the skin. Enter the sauna quietly and find a seat without disturbing others.

Löyly: The Heart of Sauna Etiquette

Löyly — the steam created by throwing water on hot stones — is the central ritual of Finnish sauna. The person controlling the löyly should always ask "Saako heittää?" (May I throw?) before adding water. Adding too much water or throwing without permission is considered disrespectful. In group settings, löyly should be applied in moderation to accommodate varying heat tolerances. Never throw water on the stove when others are about to leave — wait until the next round.

Conversation and Conduct

The sauna is a space for relaxation and quiet reflection. Conversations should be soft and respectful. Avoid loud voices, controversial topics, and business discussions. Brief, gentle conversation among friends is acceptable, but silence is equally welcomed. Never stare at others — the sauna is a non-judgmental space. Avoid making comments about others' bodies.

Cooling Down and Re-entry

After a sauna session, step outside to cool down. In Finland, this often involves rolling in snow or plunging into a cold lake. If you choose to cool down outdoors, be mindful of your surroundings and safety. Allow your body temperature to normalize before re-entering the sauna. Most Finns complete 2–3 rounds of sauna and cooling. After your final session, shower again and leave the sauna area clean and dry for the next person.
