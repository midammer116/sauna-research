How to Use a Sauna

Complete procedural guide to proper sauna usage covering preparation, technique, cooling, and post-session care.

Preparing Your Body for the Sauna

Proper preparation enhances both safety and enjoyment of the sauna experience. Remove all clothing or wrap in a clean towel, depending on personal preference and local customs. Remove jewelry and metal accessories, as these can become uncomfortably hot. Shower briefly to cleanse the skin and remove any lotions, oils, or cosmetics that could interfere with sweating. Dry thoroughly before entering the sauna; moisture on the skin can cause discomfort when exposed to high temperatures.

Correct Seating and Positioning

Seating position significantly affects the sauna experience. The lower benches provide a cooler environment, typically 20 to 30 degrees Celsius cooler than the upper benches. Beginners should start on the lower bench. Sitting rather than lying down allows for easier adjustment of position and more controlled heat exposure. A clean dry towel should always be placed on the bench for hygiene purposes. Sitting posture should be relaxed with feet flat on the bench or floor.

Managing Heat Exposure and Löyly

The application of löyly, or water on the sauna stones, is a defining element of the Finnish sauna experience. A small ladle of water, approximately 100 to 200 milliliters, poured over the heated stones creates a brief pulse of steam that increases perceived heat and humidity. The steam should be directed toward the lower part of the stones to distribute evenly. Löyly should be applied in moderation, particularly for beginners, as excessive steam can become overwhelming.

The Cooling Phase: Methods and Benefits

The cooling phase is as important as the heating phase in the sauna cycle. After exiting the sauna, allow the body to cool gradually. Sitting in a cool room at room temperature for 10 to 15 minutes allows core temperature to normalize naturally. More advanced users may take a lukewarm or cool shower, or for those with experience, a brief immersion in cool water. The contrast between heat and cold stimulates circulation and enhances the overall physiological response.

Post-Session Hygiene and Rehydration

After completing the final cooling phase, a thorough warm or lukewarm shower removes sweat and impurities from the skin. Pat skin dry rather than rubbing vigorously. Rehydration with water or an electrolyte beverage is essential to replace fluids lost during the session. Light stretching can complement the relaxation effects of the sauna. Allow 15 to 30 minutes of rest before engaging in demanding physical or mental activity.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Common mistakes in sauna usage include staying too long, particularly during the first session; failing to hydrate adequately before and after; using alcohol before or during sauna bathing; wearing synthetic fabrics that can melt or cause skin irritation; and using the sauna immediately after a heavy meal. Avoiding these errors ensures a safer, more pleasant sauna experience and maximizes the health benefits of regular practice.
