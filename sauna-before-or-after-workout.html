Sauna Before or After Workout

Learn the optimal timing for sauna use around your training sessions to maximize recovery, performance, and safety.

Sauna Before Exercise: Benefits and Risks

Using a sauna before a workout can increase core temperature, improve joint mobility, and enhance mental focus. The heat exposure promotes blood flow to muscles and connective tissues, potentially reducing the risk of strain during subsequent activity. However, pre-exercise sauna use also carries risks. Dehydration from sweat loss can impair endurance performance and increase the risk of heat-related illness during exercise. A 2020 study in the Journal of Science and Medicine in Sport found that sauna exposure immediately before training reduced time to exhaustion by 5–8% in endurance athletes due to elevated core temperature and cardiovascular strain.

For most individuals, a short sauna session of 10–15 minutes at moderate temperature (70–80 °C) followed by adequate cool-down and hydration may be acceptable before low-to-moderate intensity exercise. However, for high-intensity or endurance training, pre-workout sauna use is generally not recommended.

Sauna After Exercise: The Gold Standard

Post-workout sauna use is widely regarded as the optimal timing for most fitness goals. After exercise, the body is in a state of elevated metabolic activity, and heat stress can enhance recovery by promoting vasodilation, increasing nutrient delivery to muscles, and facilitating the removal of metabolic waste products such as lactate. The combination of exercise-induced and sauna-induced growth hormone release may also amplify anabolic signaling.

A 2015 study published in the Journal of Human Kinetics demonstrated that a 30-minute sauna session after resistance training significantly reduced markers of muscle damage and soreness at 24, 48, and 72 hours post-exercise. Participants also reported improved perceived recovery and readiness for subsequent training sessions. These benefits make post-workout sauna use a valuable tool for athletes, bodybuilders, and recreational exercisers alike.

Hydration Considerations

Regardless of timing, hydration is the most critical factor when combining sauna use with exercise. A typical sauna session can result in fluid loss of 0.5–1.0 liters through sweating. When combined with exercise-induced fluid loss, the risk of dehydration is compounded. Weigh yourself before and after sauna sessions and consume 1.5 times the weight lost in fluid within 2 hours. Electrolyte replacement may also be beneficial, particularly for individuals who sweat heavily or exercise in hot environments.

Practical Recommendations

For most individuals, sauna use after training provides the best balance of benefits and safety. If you choose to use the sauna before exercise, limit sessions to 10–15 minutes at moderate temperatures, hydrate thoroughly, and allow at least 15–30 minutes of cool-down before training. For post-workout sauna, aim for 15–25 minutes within 30 minutes after exercise. Listen to your body — dizziness, nausea, or excessive fatigue are signs to shorten or skip the session.
