Sauna Tips

Practical advice for maximizing your sauna experience — from preparation to post-session recovery.

Preparation Tips

Prepare your body for sauna by hydrating with 500 ml of water in the hour before your session. Avoid heavy meals immediately before sauna, as digestion diverts blood flow away from the skin and can cause discomfort in the heat. Shower thoroughly to remove lotions, perfumes, and oils. Remove all jewelry, as metal heats quickly and can cause burns. Enter the sauna with a clean, dry towel to sit on — this is both hygienic and practical, as it prevents direct contact with hot surfaces.

During the Session

Sit or lie comfortably on the towel. If you are new to sauna, start on a lower bench where temperatures are milder. Breathe deeply and slowly through your nose to help your body adapt to the heat. Stay for 5–15 minutes depending on your comfort level. If you feel dizzy, nauseous, or uncomfortable at any point, exit immediately and cool down. Never push through discomfort — the sauna is about relaxation, not endurance. When you add water to the stones (löyly), do so in moderation and be considerate of others.

Cooling Down

After your sauna session, cool down gradually. A cold shower, plunge pool, or rolling in snow (if available) are traditional methods. If you are new to cold exposure, start with a brief cool rinse and gradually increase duration. Allow 5–15 minutes between rounds for your body temperature to normalize. Always listen to your body — if you feel dizzy, sit or lie down and hydrate.

Post-Sauna Care

Rehydrate thoroughly after your session. Weigh yourself before and after to estimate fluid loss. Consume 1.5 times the weight lost in fluid within 2 hours. A light snack containing electrolytes can help restore balance. Rest for 10–15 minutes after your final round before resuming normal activities. Avoid alcohol, caffeine, and heavy meals immediately after sauna. Schedule your sauna sessions at a time when you can rest afterward — evening sessions often work well as they promote relaxation and better sleep.

Frequency and Consistency

Consistent sauna use produces the most significant health benefits. Aim for 3–4 sessions per week for general health, and up to daily sessions for therapeutic purposes. Do not use the sauna if you are ill with a fever, have an active infection, or have consumed alcohol. If you have a medical condition, consult your healthcare provider before starting a sauna routine.
