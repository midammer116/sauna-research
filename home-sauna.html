Home Sauna

Your comprehensive guide to choosing, installing, and enjoying a home sauna — from types and costs to health benefits and maintenance.

Types of Home Saunas

Home saunas come in several varieties, each with distinct characteristics. Traditional Finnish saunas use a stove to heat stones and produce löyly when water is thrown on them. These can be wood-fired or electric, with electric being more common in modern homes due to convenience. Infrared saunas use infrared heaters that directly warm the body without heating the air significantly. Infrared models operate at lower temperatures and may be easier to install in existing rooms without extensive ventilation. Steam saunas (steam rooms) generate moist heat using a steam generator and operate at lower temperatures but near 100% humidity.

Within these categories, home saunas can be pre-fabricated kits, custom-built rooms, or portable units. Pre-fabricated kits offer the most accessible entry point for homeowners, providing all necessary components in a package that can be assembled in a weekend. Custom-built saunas offer complete design flexibility but require more planning and investment. Portable units, including infrared tents and small cabin designs, are suitable for apartments and renters.

Installation Considerations

Installing a home sauna requires attention to several factors. Indoor saunas need proper ventilation, moisture control, and appropriate electrical supply. Outdoor saunas require weatherproof construction and careful siting. Flooring should be water-resistant and sloped to a drain if possible. Wall materials should be non-toxic and heat-resistant — cedar, hemlock, and spruce are traditional choices. Proper insulation ensures efficient heating and consistent temperatures. Always consult local building codes and consider professional installation for electrical work.

Cost and Value

Home sauna costs vary widely by type and size. Pre-fabricated kits for 2-person saunas start at approximately €2,000–€5,000. Custom-built saunas range from €5,000–€15,000 or more, depending on materials and complexity. Infrared sauna kits are generally less expensive, starting around €1,500. Operational costs include electricity (typically €20–€60 per month for regular use) and maintenance supplies. Despite the initial investment, a home sauna can add value to your property and provides ongoing health benefits that offset the cost over time.

Maintenance and Care

Proper maintenance extends the life of your home sauna. After each use, ventilate the sauna by opening the door and any windows. Wipe down benches and walls with a damp cloth periodically. Use a mild soap solution for deeper cleaning every few months. Avoid chemical cleaners that can leave toxic residues in the heated environment. Inspect the heater, stones, and electrical components annually. Replace sauna stones every 1–2 years as they can crumble from thermal stress. With proper care, a well-built home sauna can last 20–30 years or more.
